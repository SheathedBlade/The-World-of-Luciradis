**Lyntoa Village** was a small coastal settlement within the former [[Monarchy of Stalwryn|Kingdom of Stalwryn]], situated along the cliffs near the [[Port of Anahymn]]. Its position along established routes connecting the surrounding countryside with the southern coast made it a familiar stopping point for merchants and travelers.

Despite its modest population, Lyntoa maintained an unusually close relationship with the Stalwryn monarchy. Under a longstanding agreement with the royal family, the village received privileges and protection in exchange for obligations associated with the [[Rite of Continuance]], a ritual performed at the [[#Well of Dreams|Well of Dreams]].

The village's population was predominantly human, although historical records also document inhabitants of other ancestries. Among these was an elven merchant family recorded in the kingdom's census, whose presence reflected the commercial connections extending beyond Stalwryn's borders.

Lyntoa is particularly remembered for its association with the Varens monarchy and the failed Rite of Continuance in 5277 ASC. The incident resulted in the death of Prince [[Karos Varens]], the apparent death of [[Amara Illustros]], and the departure of [[Arzen Lynos]], whose subsequent actions would profoundly influence the history of Stalwryn and the wider world.

## Etymology

The name **Lyntoa** commemorates **Diamant Lyntoa**, an early inhabitant of the settlement who became associated with the establishment of its longstanding agreement with the Stalwryn monarchy.

According to historical accounts, Diamant volunteered as the donor for the first recorded [[Rite of Continuance]] on Convergence's 14th, 751 ASC. His sacrifice preserved the life of Stalwryn Varens, an early monarch of the kingdom, and established a precedent that would shape the village's relationship with the royal family for generations.

The settlement subsequently adopted his name in recognition of his sacrifice. Its earlier designation, if one existed, has not been preserved in surviving records.

## Geography and Environment

Lyntoa occupied an elevated stretch of coastline within the southern territories of Stalwryn, overlooking the waters near the Port of Anahymn. Its position upon the coastal cliffs offered views of the surrounding sea and countryside, while established routes connected the village with nearby settlements and the kingdom's wider transportation network.

The surrounding landscape consisted of coastal uplands, stretches of woodland, and terrain suitable for small-scale settlement. Although the cliffs limited direct access to portions of the shoreline, the village's proximity to established trading routes encouraged the passage of merchants and travelers.

Lyntoa's comparatively remote location distinguished it from the larger urban centers of Stalwryn. Nevertheless, its connections with Anahymn and the royal capital of [[Merastel]] provided access to goods, services, and travelers from beyond the immediate region.

## History

### Early Settlement and the Royal Agreement

The origins of Lyntoa can be traced to a small community established along the coastal uplands of southern Stalwryn. Its earliest inhabitants relied upon the resources of the surrounding countryside and the commercial opportunities afforded by nearby routes leading toward the Port of Anahymn.

Over successive generations, the settlement developed into a familiar stopping point for merchants and travelers. Although it remained modest in size, its position between inland communities and the southern coast encouraged commercial activity beyond what might otherwise have been expected of a village of its population.

Lyntoa's history became closely intertwined with that of the Stalwryn monarchy through an agreement concerning the treatment of a rare spiritual affliction known as **[[Soul Dissociation]]**, historically referred to as _the Waning_.

The condition progressively weakened the connection between an individual's soul and physical body, eventually resulting in death if left untreated. Although conventional medical and arcanic treatments could alleviate certain symptoms, the Rite of Continuance offered an extraordinary means of preserving the afflicted individual's life through the sacrifice of another.

The agreement established obligations between the village and the royal family, under which Lyntoa provided donors for the ritual when circumstances required it. In return, the monarchy extended privileges and protection to the settlement, strengthening a relationship that would persist across numerous generations.

The first recorded performance took place in 751 ASC, when Diamant Lyntoa volunteered to save the life of Stalwryn Varens. His sacrifice became a defining event in the village's recorded history, and the settlement subsequently adopted his name.

Although the arrangement endured for centuries, the ritual itself remained exceptionally rare, with only a handful of documented performances throughout the kingdom's history.

### Life Under the Stalwryn Monarchy

Lyntoa retained much of its character as a small trading settlement throughout the later centuries of Stalwryn's existence.

Its commercial relationships with nearby communities brought merchants and travelers through the village, while its royal privileges provided opportunities and protections not commonly associated with settlements of comparable size.

The village was home to a predominantly human population, although its inhabitants were not entirely isolated from the wider world. Records from the later Varens monarchy document the presence of an elven merchant family whose commercial activities connected Lyntoa with the [[Ferros Republic]].

Among the children raised in the village were **Arzen Lynos** and **[[Rietta Aschlera]]**, whose friendship developed during their early years. Lyntoa was also home to **Amara Illustros**, a former traveler who had settled there and become a caretaker to Arzen's family.

For much of its history, daily life continued around the village's ordinary activities. Yet the obligations imposed by its royal agreement remained a persistent aspect of its relationship with the kingdom, even during generations when the Rite of Continuance was not performed.

### The Final Rite of Continuance

On Horizon's 19th, 5277 ASC, Lyntoa became the site of the final recorded attempt to perform the Rite of Continuance.

The intended beneficiary was **Prince Karos Varens**, the only son of King [[Leoris Varens]] and heir to the Stalwryn throne. Suffering from Soul Dissociation, Karos faced an increasingly unfavorable prognosis, prompting the monarchy to invoke the longstanding agreement with Lyntoa.

**Arzen Lynos**, then thirteen years of age, was selected to serve as the ritual's donor. His selection was made against his wishes, and although his family opposed the decision, the obligations imposed upon the village placed considerable pressure upon those involved.

The ritual did not proceed as intended.

**Amara Illustros**, unwilling to permit Arzen's sacrifice, deliberately interfered with its execution. Her intervention prevented the ritual from fulfilling its intended purpose, resulting in Karos's death and Amara's apparent demise.

The incident left Lyntoa deeply divided. Resentment spread among those who had supported the sacrifice, with some directing their hostility toward Arzen despite his unwilling participation. Others opposed the treatment of the boy and assisted in arranging his escape from the village.

Arzen's departure marked the end of his childhood in Lyntoa. Although he would later travel throughout Stalwryn and develop relationships with individuals far beyond his home, the events surrounding the failed ritual remained central to the circumstances of his early life.

### Decline and Historical Legacy

The failed ritual in 5277 ASC marked the end of Lyntoa's documented participation in the Rite of Continuance.

The death of Prince Karos deprived Stalwryn of its only recognized royal heir, while the subsequent destruction of the kingdom brought an end to the monarchy under which the agreement had been maintained.

With the loss of the royal family and the political institutions that had sustained its privileges, Lyntoa's longstanding relationship with Stalwryn ceased to serve its original purpose.

Although the village's subsequent circumstances are incompletely documented, its historical importance endured through the accounts of the Rite of Continuance and the individuals whose lives were altered by its final performance.

Lyntoa remains closely associated with Arzen Lynos, whose departure from the village marked the beginning of a journey that would eventually extend far beyond the borders of Stalwryn.

## Culture and Society

Historically, Lyntoa's inhabitants maintained the customs and social relationships of a small coastal community. Its modest population encouraged familiarity among residents, while the movement of merchants and travelers introduced goods and practices from surrounding regions.

The village's proximity to established trading routes distinguished it from more isolated rural settlements. Commercial visitors provided opportunities for exchange, and the presence of families originating beyond Stalwryn reflected the connections maintained through these activities.

The longstanding relationship with the monarchy also influenced the community's social expectations. The privileges associated with the royal agreement were accompanied by obligations that could place individual inhabitants at odds with the perceived interests of the village.

These tensions became particularly evident during the final Rite of Continuance, when disagreements over Arzen's intended sacrifice divided the community. The incident demonstrated the conflicting loyalties that could arise between the village's obligations to the monarchy and the welfare of its own inhabitants.

## Places of Interest

### Well of Dreams

The **Well of Dreams** was a ritual structure established by Lyntoa's early inhabitants under an agreement with the Stalwryn monarchy. Although its outward appearance was that of a well, the structure served a specialized purpose associated with the [[Rite of Continuance]].

The ritual was intended to treat Soul Dissociation, a condition that progressively weakened the connection between the soul and physical body. By sacrificing a donor, the Rite of Continuance sought to transfer the donor's soul to an afflicted recipient, reinforcing the recipient's failing spiritual connection and preserving their life.

The Well of Dreams served as the site through which this process was performed. Historical accounts describe the donor entering the Well as part of the ritual, after which the transfer of the soul was believed to occur through mechanisms associated with the [[Stairs of Wanderlust]], the realm traversed by departed souls.

The structure was employed only rarely throughout Stalwryn's history. Its most notable recorded performances were the sacrifice of Diamant Lyntoa in 751 ASC and the failed ritual intended to preserve Prince Karos Varens in 5277 ASC.

The latter incident marked the end of the Well's documented use for its original purpose. The subsequent fall of Stalwryn brought an end to the royal agreement under which its operation had been maintained.

Later investigations revealed that the Well had become associated with events extending far beyond its intended function. Following the expedition to Cicatri Ciel, the site was no longer regarded as a place of active ritual practice.

Its present physical condition and the extent to which its former properties persist remain undocumented.
