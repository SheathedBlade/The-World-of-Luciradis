> **Astos Calendar**
>
> *Compiled by K. Myrai, Head Archivist of Oracle's Vision*

The **Astos Calendar** is the principal system of timekeeping employed throughout Luciradis, providing a common framework for the measurement of years, the organization of months, and the documentation of historical events. Its name commemorates [[Astos Yerazel]], the original Aurelant of the Convergence, whose contributions to the early development of timekeeping predate his acquisition of Authority.

Historical accounts describe Astos as a skilled artisan of the Era of Genesis, traditionally credited with the development of an early sundial. Although methods of observing the passage of time existed long before his invention, his work introduced a more consistent means of measuring and dividing the day into regular intervals.

The calendar itself was established following the Scarring, through efforts traditionally attributed to the earliest Ascendants of the [[The Aurelants#The Convergence|Convergence]] and [[The Aurelants#The Axiom|Axiom]]. Drawing upon earlier methods of timekeeping, these figures contributed to the development of a standardized system intended to reconcile differing chronological traditions and provide a common reference for historical documentation.

The system subsequently gained widespread recognition and became the principal calendar employed throughout Luciradis, although regional practices and historical conventions have continued to influence its interpretation and use.

## Year Reckoning

The Astos Calendar measures years in relation to the conclusion of the Scarring, the catastrophic conflict among the original Aurelants that ended with the Sundering.

Years preceding this event are designated **BSC**, meaning _Before Scarring_, while subsequent years are designated **ASC**, meaning _After Scarring_. These designations remain consistent throughout the calendar's use and are not altered by changes in historical era.

The transition between the two reckonings is marked by the commencement of 1 ASC, traditionally associated with the conclusion of the Scarring and the Sundering. The preceding year is designated 1 BSC, with no intervening year zero in ordinary civil notation. This chronological boundary was established retrospectively during the calendar's standardization and does not necessarily correspond to the precise date upon which the Sundering occurred. Earlier historical records were subsequently reconciled with the new reckoning, allowing events predating its establishment to be expressed within the same chronology.

Although the Era of Ascendance and Era of Freedom represent distinct periods in the history of Luciradis, their boundaries do not constitute separate chronological reference points. The transition between them therefore does not reset the numbering of years, and dates from both periods continue to be expressed using the same system.

## Calendar Structure

The Astos Calendar comprises twelve months of thirty days each, producing a total of 360 days within the ordinary monthly cycle. An additional period of five days, known as **Renewal**, completes the calendar year. In leap years, this period is extended to six days, bringing the total length of the year to 366 days.

The calendar employs a system of intercalation to maintain its correspondence with the astronomical year. A sixth day of Renewal is ordinarily introduced every fourth year, with rare exceptions in which the additional day is omitted to correct accumulated seasonal drift. Such adjustments are determined through recognized astronomical procedures and announced in advance, preserving the calendar's predictability without altering the fixed duration or ordering of its twelve months.

The beginning of the calendar year is traditionally aligned with the **spring equinox** of Luciradis's northern hemisphere, placing Creator's first day near the transition toward longer daylight hours. This arrangement reflects the calendar's historical origins and symbolizes the beginning of a new cycle, complementing the period of Renewal that concludes the preceding year.

The remaining seasonal transitions occur at broadly regular intervals throughout the year, with the summer solstice, autumn equinox, and winter solstice corresponding approximately to the transitions between successive groups of three months.

As Luciradis experiences opposing seasonal cycles across its hemispheres, the same calendar months correspond to different seasons in [[Locations/Cicatri Ciel/index|Cicatri Ciel]] and [[Locations/Nova Ciel/index|Nova Ciel]]. Although the calendar's original seasonal associations reflect its northern origins, its chronological structure remains consistent throughout the world.

## Weekly Cycle

The Astos Calendar divides each of its twelve months into five weeks of six days, establishing a consistent weekly structure throughout the ordinary calendar year. As each month contains precisely thirty days, its first day invariably corresponds to the beginning of a new week.

The days of Renewal fall outside the ordinary weekly cycle and are not assigned conventional weekdays. Following the conclusion of this period, the weekly sequence resumes with Creator's first day, marking the beginning of both a new month and a new calendar year. This arrangement remains unchanged in leap years, as the additional sixth day of Renewal does not affect the alignment of the succeeding year's weeks.

Although the formal weekly cycle does not extend through Renewal, activities requiring uninterrupted scheduling, including essential services and certain commercial operations, continue to observe regular intervals according to their respective requirements.

The days of each week are identified by their ordinal positions, from the first through the sixth, while the five weeks of each month are numbered in the same manner. Because the weekly arrangement remains consistent across all twelve months, the weekday corresponding to any numbered date can be determined without additional notation. Weekday references are therefore most commonly employed in recurring schedules and ordinary conversation, rather than as part of formal calendar dates.

## Renewal

**Renewal** is the five-day period separating the conclusion of Convergence from the beginning of Creator in the following year. Although formally included within the calendar year, Renewal is not considered a thirteenth month and is distinguished from the twelve months associated with the Aurelants.

The period is traditionally associated with reflection, renewal, and preparations for the coming year. Its placement at the conclusion of the annual cycle reflects the calendar's historical development following the Scarring, although the customs and significance attributed to Renewal vary among the civilizations of Luciradis.

Each day of Renewal is numbered independently, ordinarily from the first through the fifth. In leap years, a sixth day is observed as an additional calendar day before the commencement of the new year.

Births, deaths, and other events occurring during Renewal are recorded according to their actual dates. Individuals born on the sixth day of Renewal retain that date in official records, although their birthdays are customarily observed on the fifth day of Renewal in ordinary years. The legal treatment of such anniversaries may vary according to local civil conventions.

Although Renewal is widely recognized as a period of preparation for the coming year, its observance is not uniform. Local customs, professional responsibilities, and institutional practices determine the extent to which ordinary activities are suspended or modified during its duration.

## Date Notation

The original convention for expressing dates followed the form _"N-th Day of the Aurelant, from the Year X (BSC/ASC)"_, identifying the numbered day, its corresponding month, and the year relative to the Scarring.

Following the [[Cicatri Emigration]] and the subsequent development of [[Locations/Nova Ciel/index|Nova Ciel]], a shortened convention became increasingly common. Contemporary dates are ordinarily expressed as _"Aurelant's N-th, X BSC/ASC"_, retaining the information of the earlier convention while reducing its length. Numerical notation places the day before the corresponding month and year, omitting the reckoning designation only where it is otherwise understood from context.

For example, the sixteenth day of Horizon in the year 10110 ASC is written as **Horizon's 16th, 10110 ASC (16/05/10110)**.

The older notation remains recognizable in historical records and formal documents, although the shortened convention is generally preferred in contemporary writing.

Dates occurring during Renewal follow the same general conventions as those of the twelve months. For example, the third day of Renewal in 10110 ASC is written as **Renewal's 3rd, 10110 ASC (03/R/10110)**. The letter R distinguishes Renewal from the twelve numbered months without treating it as a thirteenth month.

## Civil Timekeeping

The Astos Calendar is accompanied by a standardized division of the day into twenty-four hours, each comprising sixty minutes of sixty seconds. These units provide a common basis for ordinary schedules, scientific measurements, and the coordination of activities across different regions of Luciradis.

Although early methods of timekeeping relied upon observations of the sun and other celestial bodies, advances in mechanical and arcanic instrumentation gradually enabled more precise measurement of time. Contemporary institutions employ standardized clocks and timekeeping procedures, while local civil time is adjusted according to geographical location.

The widespread development of transportation, communications, and international scientific cooperation has encouraged the adoption of common timekeeping standards. Nevertheless, regional conventions concerning the expression of time and the organization of daily activities continue to vary among the civilizations of Luciradis.
