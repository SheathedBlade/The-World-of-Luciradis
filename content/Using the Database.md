The Luciradis Knowledge Database provides several methods of accessing and exploring its collections. This guide outlines the principal navigation features available to readers and explains how individual entries are organized and connected.

## Finding Information

Readers seeking information on a particular subject may use the **Search** function to locate relevant entries. Search results provide direct access to the corresponding pages.

Alternatively, the **Explorer** presents the Database's collections in a hierarchical directory. Entries are organized by subject, with additional subdivisions where appropriate. Selecting a category allows readers to browse its contents, while selecting an individual entry opens the corresponding page.

For readers unfamiliar with Luciradis, the homepage provides a subject directory and a selection of introductory entries.

## Navigating Entries

Many entries contain links to related subjects, including historical events, notable individuals, geographical locations, and specialized terminology.

Selecting a link opens the corresponding entry. On devices that support hovering, placing the pointer over an internal link may also display a preview of its contents without requiring navigation away from the current page.

These references allow readers to examine unfamiliar subjects in greater detail while maintaining the context of the original entry.

## References and Connections

The Database provides several features for examining relationships between entries.

**Backlinks** identify other entries that reference the page currently being viewed. For example, if an article concerning the Empyrean Concepts references an individual, the individual's entry may list that article among its backlinks.

The **Graph View** offers a visual representation of connections between entries. Each node represents a page, while the lines between nodes indicate links. Readers may interact with the graph to examine these relationships and navigate to connected entries.

These features are intended to supplement conventional navigation, allowing readers to discover related material that may not appear within the same subject directory.

## Historical Sources and Editorial Annotations

The Database incorporates materials drawn from different historical periods, institutions, and individual contributors. While efforts are made to preserve the original context of these sources, some accounts remain incomplete, disputed, or subject to later interpretation.

Where clarification is warranted, Oracle's Vision may include editorial annotations alongside the original material. These observations are distinguished from the surrounding text and attributed to their respective contributors.

Annotations designated as **Archivist's Notes** may provide historical context, identify inconsistencies between surviving accounts, or discuss interpretations that warrant further examination.

Readers should distinguish these later observations from the historical material to which they refer, particularly when an entry concerns events for which contemporary evidence is limited.

## Recent Records

The **Recent Records** section provides access to recently published or updated entries within the Database. It offers a convenient means of discovering new material and returning to subjects that have undergone revision.

As the Database continues to expand, existing entries may be updated to incorporate new findings, additional sources, or revised interpretations.
